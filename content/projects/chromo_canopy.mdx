## Overview

**Chromo Canopy** was a first-year project for the module **03.007 Design Thinking and Innovation**. The brief was to identify problems around Changi Business Park and propose innovative solutions.

After exploring the area, our team focused on a series of underused cul-de-sac canopies. These structures shared the same design: an open circular steel frame supported by columns, with metal benches underneath. In Singapore’s climate, they offered little real shelter from sun or rain, and the metal seats became uncomfortably hot. As a result, the spaces were rarely used.

Our goal was to revitalise these spots for the main users nearby — office workers — while reusing as much of the existing structure as possible so the idea could be applied across multiple sites.

## The Problem

The canopies had several clear issues:

- Open roofs provided almost no protection from rain or strong sun
- Metal benches heated up quickly and were unpleasant to sit on
- Seating was scattered with little order or flexibility
- The all-metal look felt dull and uninviting
- There was no strong reason for people to stop or spend time there

We framed the challenge around two things: **comfort** and **interaction**. The space needed to feel better to use and more interesting to visit, without a full rebuild.

## Design Approach

We followed a design thinking process: observe, define the problem, ideate, and refine solutions that could work with the existing structure.

Key constraints we set for ourselves:

- Reuse the current steel frame and columns
- Keep changes modular and relatively easy to install
- Improve comfort in hot weather
- Give the space a clearer purpose and visual identity

## Solution

We proposed two complementary interventions.

### 1. Modular wooden seating

We designed wooden overlays that sit on top of the existing metal benches. Wood stays cooler than metal under the sun and softens the look of the space.

Connector modules let benches be joined into different layouts — for individuals, small groups, or longer shared seating. The system is modular so configurations can change by site or need, without replacing the original structure.

### 2. Dichroic glass canopy

We proposed covering the open roof with glass treated with dichroic film. This does two things at once:

- Provides real shelter from rain and sun
- Acts as a simple art installation — light passing through the film shifts colour depending on angle and time of day

The canopy becomes both more functional and more visually engaging, turning a dull structure into a small point of interest.

## My Role

I worked on the seating system and built the prototype:

- CAD: Modelled the wooden seat overlays and connector modules in Fusion 360, so the benches could be joined into different layouts.
- Woodworking: Built the small-scale wooden models by hand to test how the modules fit together and how they looked, then built a full-size prototype of the seating to check scale, comfort, and construction.

Because we couldn't modify the actual canopies at Changi Business Park, the team used mockups to simulate how the space would feel, and the wooden models and full-size prototype to test the seating design.


## Outcome

We presented the concept using mockups, small-scale wooden models, and a full-size prototype of the seating, since the real canopies couldn't be modified. Together, the modular seating and dichroic roof aimed to make the canopies more comfortable, flexible, and attractive to office workers, while building on the existing structure.

## Reflection

This project reinforced how much of design thinking happens before any design starts. Walking the site and watching how people used, or avoided, the canopies gave us a far more specific problem than we would have found from a desk, and framing it around comfort and interaction for office workers kept us from chasing ideas that sounded good but didn't fit. 
Working within the existing steel frame, the climate and our inability to modify the real site pushed us toward simpler, modular solutions, and building mockups and small-scale prototypes let us test the seating and catch problems early, which is far cheaper than finding them on site. 
It was also just fun to work with wood.
